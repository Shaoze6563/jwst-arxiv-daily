JWST/NIRCam has recovered the stellar counterparts of many ALMA-detected, formerly HST-dark galaxies, yet ALMA has started identifying dusty star-forming galaxies (DSFGs) at $z>6$ that remain extremely faint or undetected even with NIRCam. These "NIRCam-dark" DSFGs may trace a missing rapid-growth phase connecting UV-luminous galaxies at $z>10$ to massive quiescent galaxies at $z\gtrsim4$. So far, the small, heterogeneously selected NIRCam-dark sample is insufficient to determine their abundance or role in the evolution of massive galaxies. We show that a blind 1.3-mm continuum survey with ALMA2040 can provide the first high-significance census of this elusive NIRCam-dark DSFG population across $z\simeq 6 - 9$. We conduct sensitivity and survey-speed calculations requiring a $10σ$ detection of a NIRCam-dark DSFG with a dust-obscured star-formation rate of $60\,M_\odot\,{\rm yr}^{-1}$ at $z = 8$. At a like-for-like 7.5-GHz normalization, ALMA2040 will reach the required 1.3-mm RMS of $21\,μ$Jy beam$^{-1}$ about 90 times faster than current ALMA. With 100 hr on-source, ALMA2040 can cover an area of $\sim0.5$ deg$^2$ and detect $\langle N \rangle\sim16$ NIRCam-dark DSFGs over $7.5<z<9$, assuming a fiducial number density $n=3\times10^{-6}$ cMpc$^{-3}$. Dividing the survey among independent fields with deep JWST coverage would mitigate cosmic variance and enable immediate NIRCam, MIRI, and spectroscopic characterization of their environments. The $\geq512$-GHz instantaneous bandwidth expected for ALMA2040 will also detect [C II] line at a wide redshift range, supplementing the continuum discovery with redshift confirmation. Such a survey will test whether NIRCam-dark DSFGs trace a missing rapid-growth phase between the earliest UV-luminous galaxies at $z>10$ and the rapid emergence of massive quiescent systems at $z\gtrsim 4$.

arXiv 摘要 · PDF

The discovery of massive quiescent galaxies at high redshift by JWST has revealed that star formation can cease rapidly in the early Universe, yet the physical mechanisms responsible for quenching remain unknown. A key missing element is the systematic measurement in statistically representative quiescent samples of the cold gas and dust content, which are crucial for distinguishing quenching mechanisms and evolutionary pathways. Such observations are currently not feasible because state-of-the-art sub-millimeter facilities (e.g., ALMA) cannot routinely detect the faint molecular gas and dust reservoirs of quiescent galaxies, nor pinpoint outflowing molecular gas. A next-generation facility (ALMA2040) capable of providing transformative access to the cold interstellar medium of quiescent galaxies across redshift is therefore essential. In particular, it will allow the community to probe the molecular gas content of quiescent galaxies at cosmic noon and beyond by targeting the CO, [CI], and [CII] emission lines, as well as unveiling the physical conditions of the cold interstellar medium by constraining the CO spectral line energy distribution. It will also reveal the dust content of the passive population, a crucial ingredient to constrain the different quenching mechanisms. Finally, it will enable probing the molecular gas kinematics and identify molecular outflows driven by supermassive black holes. When combined with the rich legacy of JWST, ELT, and SKAO surveys, ALMA2040 will deliver a comprehensive, multi-phase view of feedback and quenching, allowing us to establish when, how, and why galaxies shut down their star formation across cosmic time.

Although less than 1 percent of the baryonic mass budget of a galaxy, cosmic dust profoundly alters the multi-wavelength appearance of galaxies across cosmic time. The (sub)millimeter wavelength regime is uniquely capable of weighing these dust reservoirs, and revealing the total extent of galaxy evolution that remains hidden behind them. With current facilities, however, doing so at $z > 8$ is largely out of reach. ALMA2040, a next-generation (sub)millimeter interferometer, is set to change this. Through an order-of-magnitude increase in sensitivity compared to ALMA, ALMA2040 is a transformational facility that will be able to routinely detect dust emission from galaxies at $z > 8$ and, through simultaneous multi-band characterization, accurately measure their dust masses, temperatures, and infrared luminosities. Combined with ancillary metallicity measurements -- including from the far-infrared lines detected by ALMA2040 itself -- this will establish the relative roles of supernovae and grain growth in building up dust in the earliest galaxies. Simultaneously, ALMA2040 will measure the fraction of star formation that is obscured by dust at cosmic dawn, and thereby the extent to which rest-UV and optical surveys miss early galaxy build-up. Without a 10$\times$ upgrade to ALMA -- comparable to the leap from the VLT to the ELT -- we will not be able to capitalize on the momentous advances enabled by JWST, and will remain blind to the cold and obscured component of galaxy formation in the $z > 8$ Universe.

Recent James Webb Space Telescope (JWST) observations have revealed fine-structure lines of ions with ionization potentials ${\gtrsim}50$\,eV, such as [Ne\,{\sc v}] and [O\,{\sc iv}], in nebular spectra of some low-metallicity galaxies, raising questions about the nature of the ionizing sources. Ultraluminous X-ray sources (ULXs) have been considered as potential contributors, but direct evidence linking individual ULXs to highly ionized nebulae is limited. Here we present JWST IFU spectroscopy of the nebula surrounding the template ULX Ho\,II\,X--1 in the low-metallicity galaxy Holmberg\,II, already known to emit He\,{\sc ii}\,$\lambda4686$ and [O\,{\sc iv}]\,25.89\,$μ$m lines. We resolve the infrared nebula on spatial scales of ${\sim}15-25$\,pc and report the first detections of [Ne\,{\sc v}] and [Ne\,{\sc vi}] in this source. We find anisotropic ionizing conditions: emission from the highest-ionization species arises from compact regions offset from the ULX toward the previously detected radio knots, whereas lower-ionization species trace the wider nebula. The preferential alignment of the highest-ionization emission with the radio axis suggests anisotropic escape of the ionizing continuum and a physical connection with jet-ejection activity. Spectral lines across the nebula are narrow and consistent with the instrumental resolution, indicating kinematically quiescent gas and disfavoring shocks as the dominant ionization mechanism. Together, these results provide direct evidence linking an individual ULX to a highly ionized nebula in a low-metallicity galaxy. These JWST observations of Ho~II~X--1 show that an accreting black hole in a low-metallicity galaxy is responsible for the extreme ionization of the surrounding nebula on ${\sim}100$\,pc scales.

作者：Lena Pless, Themiya Nanayakkara, Karl Glazebrook, Michael Maseda, Jarle Brinchmann, Tanya Urrutia
分类：astro-ph.GA
首次提交：2026-10-15

arXiv 摘要 · PDF

Lyman-$α$ emitters (LAEs) are frequently proposed as analogs of the low-mass, metal-poor galaxies thought to dominate cosmic reionization. In particular, galaxies with large Ly$α$ equivalent widths (EWs) are expected to have not experienced significant chemical enrichment. We test this hypothesis using a sample of 88 LAEs at $2 \lesssim z\lesssim 7$, selected from MUSE observations that are undetected in deep HST imaging and subsequently followed up with JWST/NIRSpec spectroscopy. Despite the unprecedented sensitivity of JWST, only 50 of the LAEs exhibit secure counterparts in available deep NIRCam imaging reaching typical 3$σ$ depths of $m_\text{AB}\sim30.3$ mag, highlighting the intrinsically faint nature of the population. Gas-phase metallicities are inferred by fitting optical emission lines with a grid of BPASS+cloudy photoionization models, while stellar masses are derived from NIRCam photometry through SED fitting. The final sample with secure stellar masses and gas-phase metallicities encompasses only 16 LAEs, occupying regions of standard emission-line diagnostic diagrams consistent with low-metallicity star-forming systems. Although the strong-line metallicity diagnostic R3 appears to decrease with increasing EW, we find no compelling evidence for a strong monotonic relation between EW and gas-phase metallicity when metallicities are inferred through full emission-line fitting. However, galaxies with stronger Ly$α$ emission tend to lie below the average mass-metallicity relation at fixed stellar masses, suggesting that they are preferentially less chemically evolved than typical galaxies of the same mass. We conclude that high-EW LAEs provide useful, though imperfect, analogs of the galaxies thought to drive cosmic reionization, and combining EW with stellar mass may provide a more effective route for identifying the most chemically primitive galaxies.

Understanding how the first galaxies assembled their stellar mass at cosmic dawn is a central frontier of modern astrophysics. JWST has now revealed an unexpected population of ultraviolet-bright galaxies at z>10, whose intense emission points towards a rapid stellar mass growth that challenges current models of early galaxy assembly. Yet, the cold gas fueling this growth remains hidden: current ALMA sensitivity restricts routine detection to only the brightest ionized-gas tracers such as [OIII] 88$μ$m, while [CII] 158$μ$m, a key coolant and gas-mass proxy of the neutral interstellar medium (ISM), is undetected beyond $z\sim8$. This leaves fundamental questions unanswered: how quickly primeval galaxies gathered cold gas and turned it into stars; what physical conditions (density, temperature, radiation field) characterized their ISM; how fast metals and dust built up; and how feedback regulated early gas assembly. Only a transformational sub-millimeter facility like ALMA2040 can address these questions, enabling systematic surveys of [CII] in the first galaxies and providing a representative census of gas fractions and depletion times. A simulated galaxy at $z=11.3$ shows that ALMA2040 not only will detect, but also spatially resolve its merging cold-gas structure in [CII] on $\sim$200 pc scales in 5 hours (below 100 pc in 10 hours). ALMA2040 will also spectrally resolve multiple lines simultaneously, akin to integral-field spectroscopy, providing the key diagnostics needed to break density-metallicity-radiation-field degeneracies and map the multi-phase ISM of z>10 galaxies. The combination of dramatically increased line sensitivity and wide instantaneous bandwidth will push the search for the first galaxies out to the earliest cosmic times, with [OIII] detectable up to $z\sim20$. ALMA2040 will thus be transformative for understanding how the first galaxies assembled at cosmic dawn.

We report the identification of a UV attenuation bump in the Black Pearl, a galaxy at a spectroscopic redshift of 9.3335 observed with JWST/NIRSpec as part of the ZENITH and EMBER surveys. This is the highest-redshift UV bump reported to date, probing dust properties only approximately 500 Myr after the Big Bang. The feature is directly detected as a broad depression in the rest-frame UV continuum and is well described by a local Drude profile, with a central wavelength of 2132+/-17 Angstroms, an FWHM of 413+/-54 Angstroms, and a peak attenuation of 0.21+/-0.02 mag. Joint spectrophotometric modeling with a flexible attenuation law independently recovers a pronounced UV bump without imposing its presence a priori, regardless of the adopted star-formation history. The modeling indicates a relatively low-mass, actively star-forming galaxy, with a logarithmic stellar mass of 8.35 (+0.09/-0.08) in solar units, a star-formation rate of 2.36 (+0.54/-0.42) solar masses per year, and a visual attenuation of 0.34 (+0.12/-0.11) mag. Rest-frame optical emission-line diagnostics indicate a hard ionizing spectrum and allow for a moderate AGN contribution, but provide no evidence for a powerful AGN capable of dominating the observed UV continuum. The bump is blueshifted by 43+/-17 Angstroms (approximately 2.5 sigma) relative to the canonical Milky Way 2175 Angstrom feature and by 127 +/- 17 Angstroms relative to the few UV bumps currently known at redshift approximately 7, while its width is consistent with the broad distribution measured along Milky Way sightlines. The displaced central wavelength therefore provides the clearest evidence for a nonstandard UV-bump profile at redshift 9.3335, pointing to substantial diversity in the properties of UV-bump carriers at early cosmic times, with both PAHs and small graphite grains remaining plausible candidates.

Astrophysics seeks to explain how galaxies form and evolve through the interplay of stars, gas, and dust. Giant molecular clouds (GMCs), the main reservoirs and sites of star formation, link the cold interstellar medium to the birth of stars and stellar structures. Their properties reflect galactic disk conditions, and their evolution is shaped by stellar feedback and host-galaxy dynamics. Studying GMCs across cosmic time is therefore key to understanding how gas turns into stars and drives galaxy evolution. Hubble Space Telescope and James Webb Space Telescope observations reveal compact star-forming clumps in galaxies from Cosmic Noon to the Epoch of Reionization ($z\approx6$), but their link to the molecular gas reservoir is still unclear. We do not yet know how GMC properties vary with redshift and environment, how molecular gas, dust, and young stars are arranged, or whether clumps form in unstable disks or disturbed systems. Addressing these questions requires spatially resolved observations of the cold interstellar medium at the scales probed by optical/near-infrared facilities. ALMA2040, a transformational next-generation sub-mm facility, will provide the sensitivity and angular resolution needed to detect individual GMCs in representative lensed and unlensed galaxies at $z>1$. CO and [CII], complemented by dust continuum and molecular-gas kinematics, will measure the molecular gas reservoir, its physical conditions, and its dynamical state. Together with JWST and the ELT, ALMA2040 will connect cold gas, dust, and stellar structures across cosmic time. ALMA2040 will revolutionize our understanding of (i) the properties and evolution of GMCs and their connection to star-forming clumps, (ii) the spatial interplay between molecular gas, dust, and young stars, and (iii) the dynamics, survival, and formation of GMCs and stellar clumps.

We analyze co-spatial HST/ACS and JWST/NIRCam time-series photometric observations of RR Lyrae in the low-metallicity dwarf galaxy Leo P. This study provides a new opportunity to explore the properties of and potential for metal-poor RR Lyrae studies with JWST using well-understood relationships from past HST work. We identify 20 RR Lyrae in the HST imaging and 15 RR Lyrae in the JWST imaging. We determine the periods, amplitudes, and mean magnitudes for each of the RR Lyrae. 10. How Little Red Dots Turn Blue: Evolution of Kelvin-Helmholtz Contracting Black Hole Envelopes and Observational Diagnostics

相关度 5

匹配词：JWST, James Webb Space Telescope, galaxies, star formation
作者：Zihao Wang, Kohei Inayoshi
分类：astro-ph.GA, astro-ph.HE
首次提交：2026-10-14

arXiv 摘要 · PDF

James Webb Space Telescope (JWST) observations have revealed compact, red-colored, broad-line emitters known as little red dots (LRDs), while a bluer counterpart population has been referred to as little blue dots (LBDs). Their physical connection remains unclear: they may represent different evolutionary stages of the same phenomenon, distinct populations, or different phases of the broader evolution of early galaxies and their black holes (BHs). We propose a model in which a rapidly growing BH is embedded in a geometrically thick gaseous envelope accompanied by concurrent nuclear star formation. The model reproduces the observed spectral energy distributions (SEDs) of LRDs and LBDs, and naturally connects the two phases through Kelvin-Helmholtz contraction of the envelope and exposure of the nuclear accretion disk, as the gas supply declines due to external stellar activity or feedback. We predict the corresponding evolutionary tracks in the UV-optical slope ($β_{\rm UV}$-$β_{\rm opt}$) plane which bridge the apparent gap between LRDs and LBDs. These evolutionary tracks start from the LRD regime and consistently exhibit a red excursion that extends toward $(β_{\rm UV},β_{\rm opt})\sim(2,-1)$ before turning toward the LBD regime. This feature is largely insensitive to variations in the model parameters that govern the envelope structure, providing a robust signature of the transitional population. The transition lasts $\sim5$-$50\,\mathrm{Myr}$, depending primarily on the BH feedback efficiency and the gas-retention efficiency of the envelope. The abundance ratio of transitional systems to LRDs therefore provides a constraint on BH feedback and gas retention during the rapid growth of early supermassive BHs.
