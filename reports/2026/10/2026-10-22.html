1. Unveiling the correlation between the UV 2175 Å dust attenuation bump, PAH emission, and gas-phase metallicity at cosmic noon

作者：L. Arriscado, I. Shivaei, L. A. Boogaard, A. Mizener, D. Calzetti, P. G. Pérez-González, F. Rodríguez Montero, G. H. Rieke, Y. Zhu, A. Battisti, A. J. Bunker, E. Curtis-Lake, T. Díaz-Santos, Z. Ji, P. Rinaldi, B. Rodríguez del Pino, S. Tacchella, A. Varo-O'Ferrall, C. C. Williams, J. Witstok

arXiv 摘要 · PDF

The UV 2175 Å attenuation bump is one of the most prominent yet poorly understood features of galaxy attenuation curves. Its main candidate carrier is polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbon (PAH) molecules, which are more prevalent in high-metallicity environments. We present the first systematic study of the relationship between PAH abundance and the UV bump in 49 galaxies at cosmic noon (1.5 < z < 2.7), examining its dependence on stellar mass, star formation rate (SFR), and V-band optical depth. For 19 galaxies, we also investigate gas-phase metallicity using oxygen abundances. We measure the bump amplitude from VLT/MUSE spectroscopy and derive galaxy properties, including the PAH-to-dust mass fraction $q_{\rm PAH}$, through spectral energy distribution fitting with Prospector, combining JWST/NIRCam and JWST/MIRI photometry. Oxygen abundances are derived from strong-line diagnostics using Keck/MOSFIRE and JWST/NIRSpec data. We find a positive correlation between bump amplitude $E_{\rm bump}$ and $q_{\rm PAH}$ (Spearman $ρ= 0.60$). UV bumps are rarely detected at low $q_{\rm PAH}$, supporting PAHs as their primary carriers. Both $E_{\rm bump}$ and $q_{\rm PAH}$ correlate with stellar mass, oxygen abundance, and optical depth, and anticorrelate with specific SFR, suggesting that PAHs and the UV bump are suppressed in low-metallicity, dust-poor, and/or intensely star-forming environments. This is consistent with PAH destruction in environments with limited dust shielding and/or strong radiation fields. A principal component analysis of $E_{\rm bump}$, $q_{\rm PAH}$, optical depth, specific SFR, and oxygen abundance explains 97% of the variance, suggesting that previously reported correlations with stellar mass reflect an underlying dependence on metallicity. Finally, variations in dust geometry may explain the wide range of bump amplitudes (0-0.5 mag) at high $q_{\rm PAH}$.

2. A combined HST and JWST analysis of the stellar population in Draco II

分类：astro-ph.GA, astro-ph.SR
首次提交：2026-10-20

arXiv 摘要 · PDF

Draco II (Dra II) is one of the faintest and most metal-poor Milky Way satellites known, yet its nature as either an ultra-faint dwarf galaxy or an extended globular cluster remains debated. We perform a photometric analysis of the stellar population of Dra II reducing deep multi-band observations from the Hubble Space Telescope (HST) and the James Webb Space Telescope (JWST). We select Dra II member stars based on their proper motions and photometric quality criteria. For the first time, optical and infrared photometry are combined to produce a stellar catalogue reaching 50 percent completeness at $F814W = 26.5$ mag (corresponding to the absolute magnitude $M_{F814W} = 10.1$ mag). The colour-magnitude diagram confirms that Dra II hosts an old stellar population with an age of $13.5_{-1.5}^{+1.7}$ Gyr at a distance of $19.2\pm0.9$ kpc. We measure an $α$-enhanced stellar population with a low mean metallicity of $[\rm Fe/H]_{CaHK} = -2.38 \pm 0.17$ dex and confirm the presence of an intrinsic metallicity spread of $σ_{[\rm Fe/H]} = 0.85 \pm 0.13$ dex. We identify 10 unresolved main-sequence binary stars in the HST catalogue with mass ratios $q > 0.5$ in the mass range $0.43<M_1/M_\odot<0.71$, corresponding to a binary fraction of $f_{bin}^{q>0.5} = 0.21 \pm 0.07$. This value is higher than those typically observed in globular clusters and lies near the upper range measured in open clusters. The stellar mass function is best modelled by a single power law with a slope of $1.53\pm0.26$ over the mass range $0.13$--$0.77\ M_\odot$. We find no evidence of mass segregation. Consistent results are obtained independently from the JWST catalogue. Combining these findings strongly suggests that Draco II is best classified as an ultra-faint dwarf galaxy rather than a globular cluster.

分类：astro-ph.GA
首次提交：2026-10-21

arXiv 摘要 · PDF

Galaxy morphology out to $z\sim 6$ provides insight into galaxy formation and evolution, but identifying rare or structurally complex systems is difficult due to observational limits and the scale of modern imaging surveys. Here, we apply AdaCLIP, a Zero-Shot Anomaly Detection (ZSAD) model built upon the Contrastive Language-Image Pretraining (CLIP) model and adapted using non-astronomical anomaly-detection datasets, to identify structurally unusual galaxies without galaxy-specific anomaly-detection training or morphological labels. We extract anomaly scores and 768-dimensional feature embeddings from 38,853 galaxies in the Cosmic Evolution Early Release Science (CEERS) and Next Generation Deep Extragalactic Exploratory Public (NGDEEP) surveys, and compare the resulting anomaly scores with parametric GALFIT and nonparametric statmorph measurements. The feature embeddings reveal structure associated with galaxy morphology and observational properties, while anomaly scores show modest trends with structural measures, including asymmetry, concentration, $M_{20}$, and size, but weaker or less consistent trends with Sérsic index, axis ratio, smoothness, and Gini coefficient. These results highlight the potential of ZSAD as a scalable tool for identifying unusual galaxy morphologies in large astronomical imaging surveys, including those anticipated from the Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope.

We present the spatially resolved star formation history (SFH) of the interacting, low-mass, gas-rich galaxy pair NGC 4485/NGC 4490, based on deep JWST/NIRCam imaging obtained as part of the FEAST program. Using resolved stars' near-infrared color-magnitude diagrams (CMDs) and the SFERA 2.0 CMD fitting code, we reconstruct the SFH of the system, including the tidal bridge connecting the two galaxies. We find that recent star formation is widespread across the system, extending beyond the main bodies of the galaxies into the tidal bridge and other off-center areas. In particular, a significant fraction of the recent star formation in NGC 4485 occurs outside its central region. All major regions of the system exhibit two prominent episodes of enhanced star formation, occurring around 0.5-1 Gyr and 10-30 Myr ago, with the tidal bridge showing the largest increase relative to its average star formation rate. These peaks in star formation activity occur a few hundred million years after the epochs of the first and second pericenter passages predicted by independent dynamical models, supporting a scenario in which repeated tidal interactions triggered the observed bursts through gas redistribution and compression. Our results place direct observational constraints on the formation history of NGC 4485/NGC 4490 and provide evidence for the important role of galaxy interactions in driving spatially extended, bursty star formation in low-mass galaxies.

arXiv 摘要 · PDF

The relative abundances of chemical elements in the interstellar medium (ISM) trace the history of star formation and chemical enrichment in galaxies. We investigate the abundance patterns of carbon (C), nitrogen (N), neon (Ne), sulfur (S), and argon (Ar) in star-forming galaxies at z=1-7, combining ultra-deep JWST/NIRSpec observations from the MARTA survey with archival spectroscopy. We assemble 244 galaxies with [OIII]4363 detections and derive direct Te-based abundances. New empirical strong-line calibrations for N/O, Ne/O, S/O, and Ar/O extend the analysis to an additional 514 galaxies. We find distinct abundance-metallicity trends. N/O is enhanced by 0.18 dex in median relative to local galaxies, with a stronger enhancement at low metallicity. Direct measurements reveal decreasing S/O and Ar/O with increasing metallicity, while strong-line measurements suggest a flattening at higher metallicities, especially for S/O. We also identify a stellar-mass dependence in the O/Ar-Ar/H plane, with lower-mass galaxies exhibiting lower O/Ar at fixed Ar/H, consistent with differences in star-formation efficiency and delayed SNe Ia enrichment. Ne/O remains approximately constant up to 12+log(O/H)=8.2, with a mild increase at higher metallicities and no significant redshift evolution. C/O tentatively increases with metallicity, broadly following the local relation. Finally, a forward model shows that observational selection affects the abundance ratios differently: it mainly restricts the accessible metallicity range for Ne/O and Ar/O, but preferentially selects galaxies with enhanced N/O at low metallicity, with a weaker effect for S/O. These results provide new constraints on chemical enrichment at high redshift and highlight the importance of accounting for selection effects when interpreting abundance-metallicity trends.

arXiv 摘要 · PDF

JWST is revealing abundant populations of high-$z$ galaxies with unforeseen properties: UV-luminous galaxies with high nitrogen enrichment, populations of very massive stars (VMSs; $>100\ M_\odot$), and early forms of massive black hole growth such as the Little Red Dots (LRDs). Here we present all these phenomena appearing simultaneously in COLA1, a luminous ($M_{\rm UV}=-21.3$) galaxy at $z=6.6$. New NIRSpec medium-resolution data reveal a blue UV continuum ($β_{\rm UV}=-2.5$) with strong emission lines including NIV] $\lambda1486$, stellar P Cygni profiles, and broad HeII $\lambda1640$. We fit the UV with stellar population models, and find that the models including VMSs (limited to $300\ M_\odot$) are statistically preferred. The best-fit stellar population has $M_*\approx10^8\ M_\odot$, and a young age of $2.4\pm0.8$ Myr. The rest-optical shows a redder continuum ($β_{\rm opt}=-1.1$) and broad Balmer lines with Balmer absorption akin to LRD spectra. By evolving the best-fit stellar population we find that the UV could dim by 1-2 mag in $\sim5$ Myr, and hence this system would be observed as a typical V-shaped LRD. We measure a supersolar $\rm log_{10}(N/O)=-0.07\pm0.05$ and subsolar $\rm log_{10}(C/O)=-0.64\pm0.11$; therefore $\rm log_{10}(N/C)=+0.58\pm0.12$. The independent density-sensitive NIV] $λ\lambda1483,1486$ and CIII] $λ\lambda1906/1909$ ratios require two zones with $n_e\gtrsim10^{6.5}$ cm$^{-3}$ and $n_e\lesssim10^{3.5}$ cm$^{-3}$, respectively. We estimate $\rm log_{10}(N/C)>1.3$ (2$σ$) for the dense zone, requiring extreme N/C enrichment mechanisms such as highly rotating $>100\ M_\odot$ VMSs, tidal disruption events, or supermassive stars. We speculate that runaway collisions of massive stars in a dense stellar cluster may have lead to the seeding of an intermediate-mass black hole resulting in the formation of an LRD.

The James Webb Space Telescope (JWST) has uncovered a previously unexplored population of faint active galactic nuclei (AGNs) in the early Universe. We present MoM-BL-z10 at $z=9.9837\pm0.0009$, identified through a systematic search for broad-line AGNs at $z\geq6$ using public JWST/NIRSpec PRISM spectra from the DAWN JWST Archive. The PRISM spectrum exhibits a broad component in H$β$ at 5.35 microns. A narrow+broad Gaussian fit yields a broad H$β$ detection at S/N = 3.87, with FWHM = $3480^{+2040}_{-950}$ km s$^{-1}$ and $L_{{\rm H}β,{\rm broad}}=1.25^{+0.33}_{-0.31}\times10^{42}$ erg s$^{-1}$. The detection of [Ne III] $\lambda3870$ and the high [Ne III]/[O II] and [O III]/H$β$ ratios further support the AGN interpretation. Its rest-frame UV and optical continuum slopes, compact NIRCam morphology, and a tentative MIRI F770W detection suggest similarities to Little Red Dots (LRDs). The broad H$β$ detection enables the first virial SMBH mass estimate at $z\sim10$. We derive $M_{\rm BH}=3.6^{+5.8}_{-1.7}\times10^7 M_\odot$ and $λ_{\rm Edd}=0.11^{+0.10}_{-0.07}$ using the standard single-epoch virial calibration and quasar bolometric correction. We also consider an alternative scenario adopting the LRD-specific bolometric correction and assuming $λ_{\rm Edd}=1$, which yields $M_{\rm BH}=2.1^{+0.6}_{-0.5}\times10^6 M_\odot$. Under either scenario, the inferred SMBH mass at a cosmic age of only 470 Myr highlights the challenge of rapid BH growth in the early Universe, suggesting super-Eddington accretion from a stellar-mass seed or a more massive seed. As the highest-redshift broad-line AGN identified to date, MoM-BL-z10 provides a new observational benchmark for understanding the early growth of supermassive black holes.
