## 1. The JWST/PASSAGE Survey: Metallicity Radial Gradients in Emission-Line Galaxy Pairs at Cosmic Noon with NIRISS

- **相关度：** 15
- **匹配词：** JWST, galaxy, galaxies, emission line, metallicity, star formation, stellar mass
- **作者：** Sijia Li, Xin Wang, Matthew A. Malkan, Tucker Jones, Vihang Mehta, Y. Sophia Dai, Matthew J. Hayes, Claudia Scarlata, Harry I. Teplitz, Benedetta Vulcani, Ayan Acharyya, Hakim Atek, Andrew J. Bunker, Farhanul Hasan, Mason S. Huberty, Zahra Sattari, Peter J. Watson
- **分类：** astro-ph.GA
- **首次提交：** 2026-10-30
- **链接：** [摘要](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41557) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.41557v1)

Using JWST/NIRISS slitless spectroscopy from the PASSAGE program, we analyze 17 spectroscopically confirmed emission-line galaxies in candidate interacting systems at $z \sim 1.7$--$3.2$. These galaxies span stellar masses of $7.3 \lesssim \log(M_*/M_\odot) \lesssim 10.4$ and global gas-phase metallicities of $12+\log(\mathrm{O/H}) \sim 7.6$--$8.7$. From spatially resolved emission-line maps, we measure radial metallicity gradients separately for each galaxy, finding values from approximately $-0.2$ to $+0.45$ dex kpc$^{-1}$. The mean metallicity gradients in bins of stellar mass and redshift are consistent with previous measurements, and their scatter is not significantly different from that of the heterogeneous literature compilation in either the metallicity-gradient--stellar-mass or metallicity-gradient--redshift relation. We find tentative evidence for an anti-correlation between the metallicity gradient and the metallicity offset from the mass--metallicity relation, with a best-fit slope of $-0.30 \pm 0.06$, possibly reflecting interaction-driven variations in star formation and chemical enrichment. Galaxies in pairs with comparable component masses tend to exhibit positive metallicity gradients, whereas galaxies in unequal-mass pairs tend to exhibit negative gradients; neither trend is statistically significant in the present sample. Overall, our results reveal substantial diversity in the metallicity distributions of candidate interacting systems at cosmic noon and provide tentative evidence that galaxy interactions may influence the redistribution of gas and metals.

## 2. Co-evolution of AGN and Host Galaxies across Cosmic Time Using JWST data

- **相关度：** 13
- **匹配词：** JWST, JADES, galaxies, galaxy, extragalactic, AGN, star formation, stellar mass, quiescent
- **作者：** Priyanka Jalan, Tomotsugu Goto, Tetsuya Hashimoto, Angel Rodriguez Barbosa, Chih-Teng Ling, Ece Kilerci, Terry Long Phan, Srivardini Ayyappan
- **分类：** astro-ph.GA
- **首次提交：** 2026-10-30
- **链接：** [摘要](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41584) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.41584v1)

Understanding the complex co-evolution of active galactic nuclei (AGN) and their host galaxies across cosmic time remains a fundamental challenge, particularly in identifying how the physical drivers of galaxy diversity evolve from the early Universe to the present day. To understand this, we use a multi-wavelength JWST catalog of 2,709 sources cross-matched from the SMILES and JWST Advanced Deep Extragalactic Survey (JADES) surveys, spanning lookback times from 0.8 to 12.9 Gyr ($0.05 <z <6$). Using Pearson correlation matrix, we find AGN luminosity ($L_{\mathrm{AGN}}$) strongly correlates ($\sim0.8$) with star formation rate (SFR) and dust luminosity ($L_{\mathrm{dust}}$), weakly correlates ($\sim0.5$) with stellar mass ($M_\star$) and anti-correlates ($\sim -0.4$) with star formation history (SFH) duration and stellar age (Age$_\star$). However, the apparent anti-correlation observed between galaxy age and AGN luminosity is driven by cosmic time rather than by a direct physical connection. Using Principal Component Analysis (PCA), we find that galaxy diversity is fundamentally governed by two principal components: AGN and galaxy activity, and stellar age. At higher redshifts, AGN accretion, star formation, and dust emission are tightly synchronized, reflecting gas-rich conditions in the early Universe. In contrast, the present Universe exhibits a clear bimodal structure in host-dominated systems ($f_{\mathrm{AGN}} \leq 0.2$), separating quiescent and star-forming galaxies. Our results demonstrate that AGN luminosity is primarily governed by the host galaxy's current cold-gas supply, of which recent star formation is a close observational tracer, with galaxy mass setting the overall potential. This highlights a co-evolution scenario in which black hole growth and star formation are tightly linked, particularly at early cosmic epochs.

## 3. The Size-Mass Relation and Inside-Out Quenching of Massive Quiescent Galaxies at $2&lt;z&lt;3$ with JWST PRIMER and MINERVA

- **相关度：** 13
- **匹配词：** JWST, PRIMER, NIRCam, galaxies, galaxy, quiescent, star formation, active galactic nucleus, spectral energy distribution, morphology
- **作者：** Vaidik Prasal, Yogesh Wadadekar, Pralay Biswas
- **分类：** astro-ph.GA
- **首次提交：** 2026-10-29
- **链接：** [摘要](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40937) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.40937v1)

We investigate the morphology, size--mass relations, and spatially resolved star formation histories of 26 massive quiescent galaxies at cosmic noon ($2 < z < 3$) in the Ultra-Deep Survey field using deep JWST NIRCam imaging from the PRIMER and MINERVA surveys. Combining 16 broadband and medium-band filters with empirical point spread function matching, we perform single-Sérsic modeling and bulge--disk decompositions with pysersic across six filters (F115W to F444W), alongside Bayesian size--mass fitting with dynesty. In the rest-frame optical (F200W), the size--mass relation exhibits a steep slope of $α= 0.59 \pm 0.12$ and effective radius of $A = 0.83_{-0.07}^{+0.08}\ \mathrm{kpc}$ at $M_* = 5 \times 10^{10}\ M_\odot$. In the rest-frame near-infrared (F356W), we derive $α= 0.56 \pm 0.13$ and a more compact radius of $A = 0.70_{-0.06}^{+0.07}\ \mathrm{kpc}$. Galaxy sizes shrink by $\approx 22\%$ from F115W to F444W, reflecting negative color gradients and centrally concentrated mass profiles. Two-thirds of the sample are bulge-dominated ($B/T \ge 0.5$), whereas one-third are disk-dominated. Central bulges are ultra-compact ($R_{e,\mathrm{Bulge}} \approx 0.49\ \mathrm{kpc}$) with negligible mass dependence, whereas outer disks extend to $R_{e,\mathrm{Disk}} \approx 1.4\text{--}1.5\ \mathrm{kpc}$. Resolved spectral energy distribution modeling with piXedfit demonstrates positive radial specific star formation rate gradients and inner regions that formed at least $\approx 0.7\ \mathrm{Gyr}$ earlier and quenched faster than their outskirts. These results confirm inside-out quenching, consistent with dissipative gas compaction assembling the dense core, active galactic nucleus feedback halting central star formation, and the compact bulge dynamically stabilizing the surviving disk against fragmentation.

## 4. From Quantum Field Fluctuations to High-Redshift AGN: Semiclassical Origins of Halo Mass Function Constraints in the JWST Era

- **相关度：** 13
- **匹配词：** JWST, galaxies, high redshift, AGN, cosmic dawn
- **作者：** Elan Yogeswaren, Amir-Homayoon Najmi, Robert K Schaefer
- **分类：** astro-ph.CO, astro-ph.GA
- **首次提交：** 2026-10-29
- **链接：** [摘要](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40846) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.40846v1)

We develop a continuous framework connecting quantum field fluctuations in the early Universe to the abundance and observable properties of galaxies and active galactic nuclei (AGN) at cosmic dawn. Beginning with quantum fields in curved Friedmann Lemaitre Robertson Walker spacetime, we follow inflationary vacuum fluctuations into the primordial curvature spectrum, linear matter power spectrum, mass variance $σ(MHz)$, mass peak height $ν(MHz)$, and the statistics of nonlinear halo formation. The Press-Schechter and Sheth-Tormen formalisms provide an analytic description of halo abundance and its dependence on peak rarity. A complementary high redshift halo mass function calibrated with the GUREFT simulations of Yung et al. (2024) provides a numerical description specifically constructed for the cosmic dawn regime probed by JWST. The comparison between these analytic and simulation calibrated prescriptions provides a direct measure of the sensitivity of predicted halo populations to the observations. We separate this cosmological supply of halos from the astrophysical and observational mappings that produce stars, ultraviolet luminosities, and active galactic nuclei. Controlled numerical experiments show that a percent level perturbation in the primordial fluctuation amplitude produces only a modest change in $σ$ and $ν$, but can strongly amplify the exponentially suppressed high $ν$ tail of the Halo Mass Function. This sensitivity increases toward high halo mass and high redshift, identifying rare cosmic dawn populations as potentially powerful probes of primordial statistics while simultaneously exposing the importance of uncertainties in halo abundance. High redshift AGN population statistics and the Ultraviolet Luminosity Function provide additional constraints on the primordial power spectrum and dynamics of primordial inflation theory.

We employ JWST/NIRSpec MSA PRISM spectroscopy together with NIRCam imaging to investigate the impact of star-formation variability on galaxy sizes for a representative sample of 51 galaxies at $z\geq4$. The OASIS survey enables us to probe low-star-formation-rate (SFR) lulling and mini-quenched galaxies down to $\log_{10}(M_\star/M_\odot)=8.3$ and $\log_{10}(\mathrm{SFR}/M_\odot\,\mathrm{yr}^{-1})<-1.6$, i.e. more than one order of magnitude below the star-forming main sequence (MS). This enables us to probe objects that are often missed by shallower spectroscopic surveys, in addition to the MS and starburst systems, yielding an SFR- and mass-representative sample at high redshift. We derive galaxy half-light sizes using ForcePho, enabling us to resolve the highly compact nature of these sources, and we analyse their star-formation histories and interstellar medium (ISM) properties with the full-spectral-fitting code ppxf. Using partial correlation coefficients we show that the anti-correlation between burstiness (as traced by the SFR ratio) and size cannot simply be explained by secondary dependencies on stellar mass or redshift. We interpret this relation as the effect of central starbursts in SFR-rising (bursting) systems, where intense central star formation reduces measured galaxy half-light sizes. This burstiness then regulates galaxy sizes at high redshift. Should the same mechanism be present at $z>10$, this could explain the size bimodality seen in the UV luminous galaxies as simply stemming from a more fundamental relationship between burstiness and size.

## 6. The assembly of dusty galaxies at $z \geq 4$: grain growth and the evolutionary origin of the dust-to-stellar-mass scatter

- **首次提交：** 2026-10-29
- **链接：** [摘要](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40790) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.40790v1)

Understanding dust evolution is essential for interpreting the properties of galaxies across cosmic time. Nearby galaxies have revealed a detailed picture of dust enrichment, but whether the same picture applies in the early Universe remains unclear. Here we explore the origin of interstellar dust in simulated galaxies at $z \sim 5$. Starting from the dust-to-stellar mass relation, we select five simulated star-forming galaxies with $SFR> 1 M_\odot yr^{-1}$ and $ log(M_\star/M_\odot) > 8$. We focus on three low-mass systems with comparable stellar masses ($log(M_\star/M_\odot) \sim 8.7$) but significantly different dust masses ($log(M_{\rm dust}/M_\odot) \sim 5.1-6.2$), to investigate the origin of the scatter in the relation. Our analysis combines outputs from the cosmological hydrodynamical simulation dustyGadget, a semi-analytical model for stellar dust production, and radiative-transfer post-processing with SKIRT. The transition to efficient grain growth occurs at lower metallicity than locally, consistent with the denser interstellar medium of high-redshift systems. Differences in dust content reflect different stages of galaxy assembly. Progressively longer star formation histories lead to greater chemical enrichment and denser ISM, promoting increasingly efficient grain growth. We therefore identify the assembly stage as the key regulator of dust growth at high redshift. Mock observations show that JWST multi-band imaging can reveal the structural diversity of galaxies within the scatter, although the reconstruction of clumpy morphologies may remain incomplete. Their dust continuum emission remains below the sensitivity of current ALMA continuum surveys.

- **链接：** [摘要](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41489) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.41489v1)

JWST has revealed that star formation in early galaxies is bursty, including episodes of violent suppression, so-called mini-quenching. But these were largely serendipitous discoveries in samples of UV-bright, emission-line-dominated galaxies, so that the quiet side of the burst cycle has remained poorly explored. With the JWST Cycle 3 program OASIS (GO 5997), we searched for such galaxies deliberately, using deep 28-hour NIRSpec/PRISM spectroscopy across the full burst cycle at z>4. We identify three mini-quenched galaxies with log(M*/Msun) = 8.3-8.9 and no detectable Balmer emission, at least 1.2-1.7 dex below the star-forming main sequence (SFMS), five lulling galaxies with weak Balmer emission above the quenching threshold, and 17 nearly-main-sequence galaxies just below the SFMS. Finding them required spectroscopy: seven of our ten photometric candidates for suppressed (mini-quenched and lulling) galaxies turned out to be nearly-MS or above the SFMS, and photometric fits overestimate their current star-formation rates and cannot distinguish lulling from mini-quenched ones. The suppressed galaxies are common: five of the eight come from tiers not selected on star-formation state, where they make up 29% of the galaxies at z = 4-5.5 and log(M*/Msun) = 8-9.5, a number density of ~1.6x10^-3 Mpc^-3 dex^-1. Simulations predict them to be an order of magnitude rarer at these masses and to occur mainly at lower masses. The suppressed galaxies are also old: Balmer-break strength and stellar age both increase as galaxies fall further below the SFMS, with light-weighted ages of 5 Myr for galaxies above it, ~30 Myr for the lulling galaxies and above 100 Myr for the mini-quenched ones. They show little stellar reddening (E(B-V) < 0.2), consistent with depleted gas reservoirs. Suppressed phases at z>4 are therefore neither rare nor brief, but a common and lasting part of early galaxy growth.

Models of bursty star formation in low-mass (10^8-10^9.5 Msun) galaxies at z>4 differ most in the suppressed phases: what drives the decline, how fast star formation falls, and how long it stays low. OASIS Paper I showed that such quiet galaxies are common and old; here we measure how they got there and how their ionized gas responds. We compare Balmer-line star-formation rates (SFRs) with 100-Myr averages from the stellar continuum, in NIRSpec/PRISM spectra of 54 OASIS galaxies at z>4, including three mini-quenched galaxies below the quenching threshold and five lulling galaxies at least 0.6 dex below the star-forming main sequence (SFMS) but still star-forming. Galaxies above the SFMS rise and fade equally often, whereas those below it are mostly fading: the lulling galaxies formed little more than a tenth of their mass in the last 100 Myr, and their decline began tens to ~100 Myr before observation. Suppressed phases thus last of order 100 Myr, as long as or longer than in most simulations, whereas recovery is much faster: few galaxies below the SFMS are rising. The ionized gas tracks these changes in star formation. [OIII]/[OII] falls from galaxies above the SFMS to the lulling ones, even at fixed mass and after dust correction, and follows the recent change in SFR (SFR_10/SFR_100) more closely than the position relative to the SFMS: two rising galaxies below the SFMS are highly ionized. This is expected if fewer and softer ionizing photons than in strongly star-forming galaxies meet gas depleted by earlier star formation. Lulling galaxies show ISM conditions distinct from those of strongly star-forming galaxies. The declines are faster than passive gas exhaustion predicts, favoring feedback that expels the gas or keeps it from forming stars. Quiet phases are thus long, feedback-driven episodes, and the ionized gas traces where a galaxy is in its burst cycle.

## 10. Cosmic merger rates and population evolution of binary black hole mergers in AGN disks

- **相关度：** 7
- **匹配词：** JWST, AGN, high redshift, metallicity
- **作者：** Maria Paola Vaccaro, Alessandro Trinca, Michela Mapelli, Lumen Boco, Raffaella Schneider, Rosa Valiante, Dominika Wylezalek

Active galactic nuclei (AGN) disks are promising sites for hierarchical binary black hole (BBH) mergers, which next-generation gravitational-wave detectors will probe out to high redshift. Predicting their contribution requires linking BBH evolution in AGN disks to the poorly constrained high-redshift evolution of active supermassive black holes (SMBHs). We introduce ARISE (AGN Rates and Intrinsic Stellar-BH Evolution), a framework that embeds semi-analytical models of BBH evolution in AGN disks into a cosmological description of the AGN number density, SMBH mass function, Eddington-ratio distribution, and nuclear metallicity. We consider a conservative AGN-abundance model anchored to observed luminosity functions, as well as Eddington-limited and super-Eddington scenarios based on the CAT cosmological model, compatible with JWST observations of high-redshift AGNs. The merger-rate density is set primarily by the disk viscosity parameter $α$: at $z = 0.2$, we find a rate of $12$--$37$ Gpc$^{-3}$ yr$^{-1}$ for $α= 0.01$ and $0.5$--$6$ Gpc$^{-3}$ yr$^{-1}$ for $α= 0.1$. The rate density peaks at $z \simeq 1.8$, $3.3$, and $3.9$ ($1.5$, $3.6$, and $3.9$) for $α=0.01$ ($0.1$) in the conservative, Eddington-limited, and super-Eddington models, respectively, and in the super-Eddington model it stays nearly flat out to $z \simeq 10$. For $α= 0.1$, AGN disks account for $\sim 2$--$40\%$ of the LVK-inferred merger rate, consistent with current upper limits from GW--AGN associations. For $α= 0.01$, they match or exceed it. The Einstein Telescope (ET) should detect $\sim 10^{5}$ ($\sim 10^{4}$) AGN-disk mergers per year for $α= 0.01$ ($0.1$). ET will test the AGN channel at redshifts where the AGN population models differ most strongly, providing independent constraints on the growth of early SMBHs.

## 11. When precise inference is wrong: Diagnosing model misspecification with applications to cosmic dawn and reionisation

- **相关度：** 7
- **匹配词：** JWST, galaxy, cosmic dawn
- **作者：** Daniela Breitman
- **分类：** astro-ph.CO, astro-ph.GA
- **首次提交：** 2026-10-29
- **链接：** [摘要](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40770) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.40770v1)

As observations of the cosmic dawn (CD) and epoch of reionisation (EoR) rapidly improve in precision, the approximate forward models used to interpret them might struggle to keep pace. Bayesian inference with a misspecified model, one that cannot represent the processes generating the observations, can yield tightly constrained, yet misleading and biased astrophysics, with different parts of the observations favouring incompatible parameter values. Model misspecification can evade a $χ^2$ goodness-of-fit test, and Bayesian model selection requires a better model, which is not always available. We therefore apply the suspiciousness, a statistic developed to quantify tensions between datasets, to CD/EoR inference as a model criticism tool that tests whether these parts can be explained by the same parameters. We first apply it to HERA 21-cm power spectrum (PS) upper limits, which are too weak to reveal misspecification, as expected, and to HST and JWST UV luminosity functions (LFs). Under our galaxy model, the UV LFs at $z\leq10$ and $z>10$ are in tension at $5.0σ$ in the Gaussian limit, a failure visible in the fits, although doubling the uncertainties of the $z>10$ observations reduces it to $2.1σ$. Finally, we analyse a mock SKA-Low AA$^\ast$ 1080 h 21-cm PS with the model that generated it and with a simpler, misspecified model. We find that the misspecified model silently fails: it fits the PS well, passing visual inspection and, for most noise realisations, a $χ^2$ test, yet its inferred astrophysics is wrong. The Bayesian evidence disfavours it, but only relative to the true model, which is unavailable for real observations. The suspiciousness finds no tension for the well-specified model, but a $\sim3.4σ$ tension for the misspecified one, strongly ruling it out without requiring a more adequate candidate model.
