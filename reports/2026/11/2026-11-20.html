arXiv 摘要 · PDF

Little Red Dots (LRDs) are compact, red sources discovered by JWST. Most show broad permitted emission lines characteristic of AGN, but other properties differ from local AGN, such as X-ray and radio weakness and an apparent scarcity of dust. If LRDs at z>4 are indeed dust deficient, the bolometric luminosities and black hole (BH) masses measured from SED fitting would be overestimated, and their red optical continuum would be intrinsic rather than reddened. Here we use rest-frame 3-27 micron JWST spectra of a sample of nearby (D<60 Mpc) Seyfert 1 (Sy1) nuclei to place constraints on the dust emission of LRDs. We normalised the individual Sy1 spectra by their broad H$α$ and 5100 A continuum luminosities and stacked them, obtaining scalings that reproduce, within a factor of three, the rest-frame near-infrared emission of a NIRSpec+MIRI stack of 249 LRDs at 2.3<z<9.3 and the near- and mid-infrared emission of a local LRD at z=0.1, with the 5100 A continuum giving the more reliable prediction when modest extinction is considered (Av<1 mag). However, simultaneously reproducing the optical and infrared SEDs of LRDs requires a more reddened accretion disc (Av ~ 2 mag) and a torus with a lower reprocessing efficiency (Ct ~ 0.1-0.2) than inferred for local Sy1 galaxies. This combination offers an alternative to BH-star scenarios that explain the optical continuum as an unreddened blackbody of T ~ 5000 K. Our model, based on local Sy1s, yields a more plausible infrared-to-optical energy balance, suggesting that LRDs are not dust deficient. From the torus model that reproduces the optical and near-infrared SED of the high-redshift LRD stack, we estimate a dust mass of $M_{\rm dust}\simeq10^4\,M_\odot$, consistent with the dust masses typical of AGN tori.
